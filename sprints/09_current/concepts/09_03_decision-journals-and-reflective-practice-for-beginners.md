# Decision journals and reflective practice for beginners

**Used by tasks:** 09_06, 09_07

## What it is
A decision journal records what you decided, why, and how sure you were, *before* you know the result. Reviewing it later separates a bad decision from a bad outcome. Reflective practice is the habit of asking structured questions after an experience.

## Why it matters here
The journal turns the guess game's probabilities into an habit the learner can inspect: "I wrote that the trend was up; what happened?" The review must be gentle and honest: no grades, no ranking, a reminder that few decisions prove little.

## Key ideas
- Write the reason first, in one or two sentences, before the outcome is visible.
- Review prompts: what did I expect, what happened, what did I miss, what would I do differently, how many decisions is this based on?
- Hindsight bias makes the past look obvious; the stored reason is the antidote.
- The disposition effect (selling winners too early, holding losers) is a known pattern a journal can reveal, but with few decisions it proves nothing.

## Common mistakes
Scoring the learner; showing a win rate without the count; letting the learner rewrite a reason after the result.

## Check yourself
1. Why store the reason before the result? 2. Why is a win rate on 6 decisions weak evidence?
