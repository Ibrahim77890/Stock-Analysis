# Task 06_10: Score, reveal and progress screens with plain-language explanations

**Sprint:** 06     **Depends on:** 06_09     **Size:** M
**Concepts to read first:** [06_03](../concepts/06_03_calibration-reliability-diagrams-and-sample-size.md), [06_04](../concepts/06_04_overconfidence-luck-and-the-permutation-check.md), [06_08](../concepts/06_08_designing-for-novices-plain-language-and-a-light-theme.md)

## Goal
The learner gets an honest, readable scorecard (what the number means, how it compares with guessing, how sure we can be) and, after the one-way reveal, learns what the episode really was and what to distrust about the data.

## Context
Blueprint 23.1 (teach distrust), 23.2 (prediction scoring, data lineage panel), 23.4 (mechanics vs interpretation). All numbers come from the gateway (which gets them from Python); the page computes nothing.

## What to do
1. **Score card:** a large score with a plain sentence: "Your score: 0.23. Lower is better. Guessing 50% every time scores 0.25." A horizontal **score meter** (0 to 0.5) marking the coin-flip line at 0.25 and the learner's score, with words at the ends ("Perfect" / "Worse than guessing"), plus the counts "Answered 12, waiting 3, not counted 1". Two headings keep **How it is computed** (mechanics) apart from **What it might mean** (interpretation).
2. **"Too few guesses" state:** below 30 answered guesses the interpretation section is replaced by a friendly callout: "You have 12 answered guesses. That is too few to say anything. Keep going: it takes about 30."; the luck panel and any better/worse-than-guessing wording are hidden.
3. **Calibration plot** (SVG written in React, no chart library): title "When you said 70%, how often were you right?"; diagonal "perfect" line; one dot per group of similar guesses at (average percent you said, share that came true), a bar for the uncertainty range, the number of guesses in each group printed next to the dot; groups with fewer than 5 guesses drawn hollow with the label "only a few guesses"; axes in percent. A table with the same numbers sits next to it and is linked with `aria-describedby`. A one-sentence reading help: "Dots below the diagonal on the right mean you were more sure than you were right."
4. **Luck panel** (only when enough guesses): "If your percentages had been matched to random outcomes, you would have scored this well or better about X out of 100 times." with X from the API, the seed in a "Show more" area, and the caution "Guesses about the same days overlap, so the real evidence is a bit weaker than the count suggests."
5. **Reveal:** a section "Ready to find out?" with an in-page confirmation ("Revealing ends this episode. Guesses that are still waiting will not be counted."). After success the result card shows: whether the episode was real or made-up; for real: the stock name, symbol, first and last real date, data source and the **Data trail** (where the numbers came from, how prices were adjusted, the survivorship note, the licence line "for your own learning only"); for made-up: the seed, the "no real day exists" sentence. Then **What this does not tell you** (4 bullets: small sample, overlapping guesses, today's survivors only, the past is not a forecast).
6. **Progress page** (`GET /me/progress`): across revealed episodes the average score, split into real and made-up episodes, with the sentence that on made-up (random) episodes no one can beat 0.25 on average, and a table by how-far-ahead. If there are no revealed episodes, an empty state explains how to get started.
7. Wording rules (checked by test): every result statement contains "in this sample" or "so far"; no "edge", "profitable", "guaranteed", "skilled" or "unskilled".
8. TradingView credit on the reveal card; footer as elsewhere.

## Where to do it
- Folder / file: `apps/web/src/` (components `ScoreCard`, `ScoreMeter`, `CalibrationPlot`, `LuckPanel`, `RevealPanel`, `DataTrail`, `ProgressPage`; copy in `src/copy/score.ts`).
- Technology: React, TypeScript, SVG.
- Database schema/ownership: none.

## Inputs
- Gateway `GET /episodes/:id/score`, `POST /episodes/:id/reveal`, `GET /me/progress`.

## Deliverables
- Score, reveal and progress screens with tests and copy that passes `check:copy`.

## Acceptance criteria / tests
- [ ] Golden: with the mocked 4-guess example (Brier 0.225) the card shows "0.225", the coin-flip 0.25, the meter positions the score left of the line, and the "too few" callout is shown; the luck panel and "better than guessing" wording are absent.
- [ ] With 30 mocked answered guesses the luck panel and interpretation appear with the mocked values and the overlap caution.
- [ ] Calibration plot: one dot per non-empty group, hollow dot with the label for groups of fewer than 5, uncertainty bars, counts printed; the table values equal the props (test compares them).
- [ ] Wording test over all three screens and several inputs: required phrases present, forbidden words absent.
- [ ] No identity before reveal (DOM scan on the score screen); after a successful reveal the identity appears; a failed reveal (mock 409 or 500) shows a friendly error and keeps the previous state.
- [ ] Accessibility checks in tests: every control named, the plot has a text alternative, up/down and good/bad conveyed with words or shapes as well as colour, headings in order.
- [ ] Copy check passes (average reading ease 60 or more, no sentence over 25 words).
- [ ] `npm run build`, `npm test`, `npm run check:api` pass.
- [ ] Standing invariants: reconciliation (answered + waiting + not counted equals the total shown), reproducibility (same seed text and same luck number on reload with mocked data).

## Out of scope
- Sharing or exporting results, leaderboards, trade journal, lessons.

## Notes for the implementer
If a number is `null`, show a dash and say why in words. Never turn a probability or p-value into a verdict.
